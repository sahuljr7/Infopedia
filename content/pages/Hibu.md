Hibu is a U.S.-focused provider of digital marketing services for small and medium-sized businesses (SMBs). Its offering brings together services such as websites, listings, reviews and reputation management, search and social advertising, search engine optimization (SEO), and customer communication tools. Hibu presents **Hibu One** as a platform for coordinating these services with its marketing expertise. [Hibu One](https://hibu.com/hibu-one)

## What Hibu does

In practical terms, Hibu helps local businesses establish and manage their online presence and attract prospective customers. Its services can include:

| Area | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Digital presence | Website development, business listings, reviews, and reputation management |
| Performance marketing | Search engine marketing (SEM), SEO, mobile, social, and display advertising |
| Customer communications | Marketing automation, email, and text messaging |
| Measurement | Campaign and lead reporting |

Hibu also markets AI-supported search optimization and advertising across channels such as Google, Facebook, Amazon, and Bing. Channel availability and campaign configuration depend on the services selected. [Hibu paid advertising](https://hibu.com/campaign/paid-home)

The exact products and services available depend on the customer and package. Hibu describes its offering as a subscription-based, outsourced marketing solution for SMBs. [Hibu company overview](https://hibu.com/why-hibu/about-us)

For example, a plumbing company might use a website and local listings to provide service information, advertising to reach people searching for a plumber, and call or message tracking to understand which campaigns generated inquiries. This is an illustration of how the services can fit together, not a guarantee of customer results.

## Company history

Hibu's roots are in the directory business. The company history is commonly traced to U.S. Yellow Pages operations and the wider Yell group. As consumers shifted from printed directories to web and mobile search, the business moved toward websites, search advertising, SEO, and other digital services. The history of the group connects the U.S. Hibu business with the UK Yell brand; they should not be treated as interchangeable names for every current operation. [Hibu history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hibu) · [Yell history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yell_(company))

The supplied historical account describes this sequence: Merchant Advertising Services began in 1930 and later became Yellow Book USA; BT launched Yell.com in the UK in 1996 and acquired Yellow Book USA in 1999; the combined group became Yell around 2000; Yell acquired McLeodUSA's directory business in 2002 and became publicly listed in 2003; and it expanded into Spain through a directory-business acquisition in 2006–2007. These dates and transaction values are historical summaries; consult the linked history references for context.

In 2012, Yell announced the Hibu name for the group. The UK business later returned to the Yell customer-facing brand. In 2021, H.I.G. Capital announced its acquisition of Hibu. [H.I.G. Capital acquisition announcement](https://hibu.com/company/company-news/hibu-signs-definitive-agreement-to-be-acquired-by-h.i.g.-capital)

In August 2024, Hibu announced that it had acquired RevLocal, a digital marketing provider serving SMBs and multi-location brands. Hibu said the acquisition expanded its reach in the SMB market. [Hibu acquisition announcement](https://hibu.com/site/company/company-news/hibu-announces-acquisition-of-revlocal-leading-digital-marketing-services-provider-significantly-expands-reach)

## Hibu One and the bundled-services model

Many small businesses otherwise coordinate different vendors for websites, search marketing, advertising, listings, reviews, and customer follow-up. Hibu's bundled model aims to bring several of those activities together through Hibu One and related services. Hibu describes the platform as AI-powered and supported by local marketing expertise. [Hibu One](https://hibu.com/hibu-one)

This combination is different from buying a standalone software subscription: the offer combines technology with managed marketing services and advertising support. The specific package, channels, and level of service vary, so customers should consult the current product terms for details.

## Market and competitors

Hibu competes across several categories rather than in one identical product market. Depending on the service being compared, alternatives can include website builders and providers, local SEO and listings platforms, advertising agencies, or reputation-management products. Names that appear in overlapping categories include Web.com, LocaliQ, Wpromote, Madwire, BrightLocal, Yext, GoDaddy, Wix, Squarespace, WebFX, Podium, Birdeye, and Reputation. This is a category-based comparison, not a claim that every company offers the same services.

| Hibu service area | Examples of adjacent providers or alternatives |
| --- | --- |
| Website creation | Web.com, GoDaddy, Wix, Squarespace |
| SEO and local listings | BrightLocal, WebFX, Yext, specialist SEO agencies |
| Paid advertising | LocaliQ, Wpromote, advertising agencies |
| Reviews and reputation | Podium, Birdeye, Reputation |
| Local lead generation and managed SMB marketing | RevLocal, Madwire, Web.com, LocaliQ |

Google and Yelp are also different from Hibu in their primary role: they are consumer discovery, search, or review platforms. Hibu sells services to businesses that may help them promote and manage their presence on third-party platforms. A business can therefore use a marketing provider and also advertise or appear on a discovery platform.

## Business model and the directory-to-digital shift

Hibu's model combines recurring business subscriptions with managed services, advertising, and marketing technology. The broader industry shift can be summarized as:

**Printed directories → online directories → internet advertising → digital marketing services → integrated marketing platforms**

The history matters because it shows how a directory business adapted as the way people find local services changed. Hibu's longstanding connection to local businesses is part of the company's account of its experience; claims about the value or uniqueness of that data should be understood as company positioning unless independently supported.
